1543

Copernican Revolution

The publication of Nicolaus Copernicus' heliocentric model fundamentally changed humanity's understanding of the universe and laid the foundation for modern astronomy and physics.

Historical Background

For centuries, the prevailing astronomical model placed Earth at the center of the universe. In 1543, Nicolaus Copernicus proposed that the Earth and other planets revolve around the Sun, challenging centuries of accepted belief.

Why It Was Revolutionary

The heliocentric model simplified planetary motion and inspired later work by Johannes Kepler, Galileo Galilei and Isaac Newton. It became one of the defining moments of the Scientific Revolution.

Legacy

Today the Copernican Revolution is recognized as one of the greatest turning points in the history of science. It reshaped astronomy, influenced physics, and changed humanity's place in the universe.
